# Production Design

## What It Is
Everything the audience SEES that isn't camera or lighting — the physical (or generated) world. Sets, locations, props, wardrobe, hair, makeup. In AI filmmaking, production design is what you describe in your prompts beyond the subject.

## Sets & Locations

### Location as Character
The best locations aren't backdrops — they tell story. A character's environment reveals who they are before they speak.

- **Cluttered apartment:** Busy mind, creative chaos, or depression (context determines which)
- **Minimalist white space:** Control, sterility, wealth, or emptiness
- **Decaying building:** Past glory, neglect, danger, time passing
- **Nature:** Freedom, vulnerability, beauty, indifference of the universe

### Environmental Storytelling
Plant details in the background that reward attention:
- A family photo turned face-down
- An eviction notice on a fridge
- Dead flowers in a vase
- Trophies gathering dust

For AI generation, these details must be in the prompt — the model won't add them automatically.

### Describing Locations for AI
Be specific and layered:
- **Bad:** "a room"
- **Good:** "a cramped studio apartment, overflowing bookshelves, warm lamplight, coffee cups on every surface, plants on the windowsill, afternoon light through dusty blinds"

Layers: architecture + objects + light + texture + atmosphere

## Props

### Significant Props
Objects that carry meaning (see Storycrafter's Significant Object framework):
- A recurring object that gains meaning over time
- An object that represents what the character wants or fears
- A symbol that the audience learns to read

### Functional Props
Objects that advance the plot:
- A letter, a key, a phone, a weapon
- Must be visible enough to read on screen — especially in AI-generated content where detail can get lost

## Wardrobe

### Costume as Character
What someone wears tells you who they are instantly:
- **Color:** Protagonist often in a distinct color vs. the world (red coat in Schindler's List, yellow in Kill Bill)
- **Formality:** Suit = power/conformity. Casual = rebellion/comfort. Uniform = role/identity.
- **Condition:** Clean = control. Worn/dirty = struggle. Mismatched = chaos.
- **Change:** Wardrobe shifts signal character transformation.

### Wardrobe for AI Consistency
In AI filmmaking, wardrobe is one of the best tools for character consistency across shots:
- Describe specific, distinctive clothing in every prompt
- Unique silhouettes help models maintain character recognition
- Simple, bold wardrobe choices generate more consistently than complex outfits

## Color in Production Design

Production design color should align with the color palette from `color-and-light.md`:
- **Warm set + cold lighting** = tension between comfort and threat
- **Monochrome environment + one color pop** = draws eye, creates symbolism
- **Environment color arc** = space changes as story changes (clean → messy, warm → cold)

## For AI Prompts

When describing production design in prompts, layer:
1. **Setting type:** Interior/exterior, specific location
2. **Architecture:** Style, era, condition
3. **Objects:** Key props, background details
4. **Materials:** Wood, metal, glass, fabric — texture matters
5. **Color palette:** Dominant colors in the environment
6. **Atmosphere:** Dust, fog, steam, light quality
7. **Time markers:** Era-specific details (technology, design style)
