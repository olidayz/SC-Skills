# Storyboarding

## What a Storyboard Does
A storyboard is a visual script — it shows what the camera sees, in order, before anything is produced. It's the bridge between written story and produced content. For AI filmmaking, it's your generation plan.

## Panel Craft

### What Goes in Each Panel
1. **The frame** — What does the camera see? Composition, subject placement, foreground/background.
2. **Action note** — What happens in this shot. Brief. "She opens the door." "Rain hits the window."
3. **Camera note** — Shot type + movement. "CU, static" or "WS, slow dolly in."
4. **Duration** — How long this shot holds (especially important for shorts/ads).
5. **Audio note** — Music cue, SFX, VO, or silence.
6. **Transition** — How we get to the next panel.

### Panel Quality Levels
- **Thumbnail:** Rough sketches, stick figures. Speed over polish. Good for blocking out sequence quickly.
- **Rough:** Basic composition clear, characters identifiable. Working storyboard.
- **Polished:** Detailed, sometimes colored. For pitch decks, client presentations.
- **AI-generated panels:** Use image generation to create storyboard frames. Fast, visual, shareable. Great for AI filmmaking pipeline — the storyboard frames can become your generation reference images.

### Pacing Through Panels
- **More panels per scene = faster pace.** Many short shots = energy, action, tension.
- **Fewer panels per scene = slower pace.** Long holds = contemplation, dread, beauty.
- **Panel size can vary:** A wide panel for a wide shot, a small panel for a quick cut.
- Match panel density to the emotional arc — tension sections get more panels, breathing room gets fewer.

## Continuity

### The 180° Rule
Draw an imaginary line between two subjects. Keep the camera on one side of that line. If you cross it without motivation, spatial relationships flip and the audience gets disoriented.

**Breaking it intentionally:** Crossing the line can signal disorientation, power shift, or surrealism. But you must cross it deliberately, usually with a bridging shot or motivated camera move.

### Eyeline Match
When character A looks at something, the next shot should show what they're looking at from approximately the same angle/height as their eyeline. Mismatched eyelines feel wrong even when audiences can't articulate why.

### Screen Direction
If a character exits frame-right, they should enter the next shot frame-left (unless you're showing time/distance has passed). Consistent screen direction maintains spatial logic.

### Match on Action
Cut during a movement — the action carries across the edit, making the cut invisible. A hand reaching in shot A continues in shot B. The motion bridges the cut.

### 30° Rule
Each new shot of the same subject should differ by at least 30° in angle or significantly in size. Smaller changes feel like jump cuts (which can be intentional — see French New Wave).

## Transitions

### Cut Types
- **Hard Cut:** Instant switch. The default. Most common. "Next."
- **Jump Cut:** Same subject, slightly different angle/time. Passage of time, unease, energy. French New Wave signature.
- **Match Cut:** Visual or conceptual similarity bridges two different scenes. Bone → spaceship (2001). Powerful.
- **Smash Cut:** Abrupt cut from quiet to loud (or vice versa). Contrast is the point. Comedy, horror, emphasis.
- **L-Cut (split edit):** Audio from next scene starts before the visual cuts. Anticipation, smooth flow.
- **J-Cut:** Audio from current scene continues into next visual. Connection, lingering emotion.
- **Cutaway:** Brief shot away from main action. Provides context, reaction, or breathing room.
- **Cross-Cut (parallel editing):** Alternating between two simultaneous scenes. Builds tension, shows connection.

### Transition Effects
- **Dissolve:** One image fades into another. Time passing, dreamlike, connection between scenes.
- **Fade to Black:** Scene ends. Finality, pause, chapter break.
- **Fade from Black:** Scene begins. New chapter, awakening, beginning.
- **Wipe:** One image slides/pushes another away. Energetic, retro, Star Wars.
- **Whip Pan:** Camera whips between scenes. Energy, speed, geographic shift.

### For AI Filmmaking
- Plan transitions in the storyboard — they affect which shots need visual continuity
- Match cuts require deliberate composition in both shots
- L/J-cuts are post-production but plan the audio overlap now
- Most AI video tools generate individual clips — stitch transitions in editing

## Storyboard-to-Prompt Pipeline
Each storyboard panel should contain enough information to write a generation prompt:
1. Visual description (what's in frame)
2. Composition (where things are placed)
3. Camera (shot type, angle, movement)
4. Light (source, quality, color)
5. Style (from locked style direction)
6. Duration (for video prompts)

Panel → prompt is a direct translation. The better the panel, the better the prompt.
