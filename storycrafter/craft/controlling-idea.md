# Controlling Idea

## What It Is
Robert McKee's concept from *Story*: the single sentence that expresses your story's ultimate meaning, stated as a cause-and-effect argument. It's not a topic ("love"), not a moral ("be yourself"), and not a logline. It's the thematic argument your story proves through action.

## The Formula

**[Value] is [achieved/lost] when [cause].**

- Value: The human value at stake (justice, love, freedom, truth, survival)
- Achieved/Lost: Does your story end positively or negatively for this value?
- Cause: What specific action or quality determines the outcome?

## Examples

**The Shawshank Redemption:**
"Freedom is achieved when a man refuses to let the system define him."

**Breaking Bad:**
"Power is destroyed when brilliance is corrupted by ego."

**Toy Story:**
"Love is secured when we accept that it's not diminished by sharing it."

**Jaws:**
"Safety is restored when a man overcomes his personal fear for the greater good."

## Why It Matters

1. **Decision filter:** Every scene should support or complicate the controlling idea. If a scene does neither — cut it.
2. **Conflict engine:** The controlling idea tells you what your antagonist represents (the counter-argument).
3. **Ending test:** Your ending is the final statement of the controlling idea. If the ending feels wrong, the controlling idea might be unclear.
4. **Unity:** In a 2-hour film or a 30-second ad, the controlling idea keeps everything pointed in the same direction.

## Positive vs. Negative Endings

**Positive (up-ending):** The value is achieved. The cause proves effective. (Most commercial stories.)
- "Justice is served when an ordinary person stands up to corruption."

**Negative (down-ending):** The value is lost. The cause fails or backfires. (Tragedy.)
- "Justice is destroyed when idealism meets systemic corruption." (Chinatown)

**Ironic:** The value is achieved but at a terrible cost, or lost but something unexpected is gained.
- "Freedom is achieved, but only through total isolation." (Cast Away)

## Finding Your Controlling Idea

If you're stuck, answer:
1. What human value is at stake in your story?
2. Does your protagonist succeed or fail in relation to that value?
3. What specific quality or action determines the outcome?

Combine into one sentence. That's your controlling idea.

## For Short-Form
Even a 30s short has a controlling idea — it's just compressed:
- A 30s ad about a running app: "Achievement is possible when you show up every day." (The whole ad proves this.)
- A 15s reel: The controlling idea might just be a feeling — but it should still be definable.

## Common Pitfalls
- Too vague ("Love conquers all" — what kind of love? Conquers what? How?)
- Confusing controlling idea with topic (topic: "war." Controlling idea: "Humanity survives war when individuals choose compassion over orders.")
- Multiple controlling ideas in one story (pick one — you can explore sub-themes, but one idea rules)
