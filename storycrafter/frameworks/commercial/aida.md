# AIDA (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action)

## What It Is
The oldest advertising framework. A persuasion funnel: grab attention, build interest, create desire, drive action. Works because it mirrors how humans actually make decisions.

## The 4 Stages

1. **Attention** — Stop the scroll. Break the pattern. You have 1-3 seconds.
   - Visual shock, bold statement, unexpected image, question
   - "What would you do with an extra hour every morning?"

2. **Interest** — Now that they're watching, give them a reason to stay.
   - Facts, story, demonstration, relatability
   - "Most people waste 47 minutes on their morning routine..."

3. **Desire** — Shift from "interesting" to "I want this."
   - Show the transformation. Social proof. Emotional payoff.
   - "Imagine walking out the door feeling ready, every single day."

4. **Action** — Tell them what to do. Now.
   - Clear CTA. Remove friction. Create urgency.
   - "Try it free for 30 days. Link in bio."

## When to Use It
- **Best for:** Direct response ads, conversion-focused content, product launches
- **Tone fit:** Persuasive, energetic, aspirational
- **Format fit:** 15-60s ads (ideal), landing page videos, product demos
- **Not ideal for:** Brand storytelling, entertainment-first content, awareness campaigns

## Timing (30s ad)
- Attention: 1-3s
- Interest: 5-10s
- Desire: 10-12s
- Action: 5s

## Example
**Dollar Shave Club (original ad):** Attention: "Our blades are f***ing great" (shock). Interest: Walk through warehouse, explaining the product. Desire: Humor + value proposition compound. Action: "DollarShaveClub.com" — clear, simple.

## Common Pitfalls
- All Attention, no Interest (flashy but empty)
- Skipping Desire (going straight from Interest to Action feels pushy)
- Weak CTA (if you don't tell them what to do, they'll do nothing)
