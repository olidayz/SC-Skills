# Before / After / Bridge

## What It Is
Show the world before your solution, show it after, then reveal the bridge (your product/idea) that gets them there. Pure visual storytelling — works even without words. The contrast does the heavy lifting.

## The 3 Parts

1. **Before** — The current painful/boring/broken state. Make it visceral. The audience should feel it.
   - Messy desk, frustrated face, broken process, wasted time
   - This is empathy: "I know where you are."

2. **After** — The transformed state. Aspirational but believable. Show the feeling, not just the result.
   - Clean desk, confident smile, smooth workflow, free time
   - This is desire: "This is where you could be."

3. **Bridge** — How you get from Before to After. This is your product, service, method, or idea.
   - Quick demo, explanation, or just the product name
   - This is logic: "Here's how."

## When to Use It
- **Best for:** Visual transformations, product demos, lifestyle brands, coaching, services
- **Tone fit:** Inspirational, aspirational, clean
- **Format fit:** Perfect for short-form video (the split-screen before/after is TikTok gold), 15-30s ads, Instagram reels
- **Strength:** Works with NO words. Pure visual storytelling.

## Visual Techniques
- **Split screen:** Before on left, After on right
- **Transition wipe:** Before transforms into After with a swipe/spin
- **Same shot, different state:** Same camera angle, different reality
- **Time-lapse:** Before → Bridge (process) → After in continuous motion

## Timing (30s)
- Before: 8-10s (establish the pain)
- After: 8-10s (show the dream)
- Bridge: 8-10s (reveal the path + CTA)

Or compressed (15s):
- Before: 3s → After: 5s → Bridge: 7s

## Example
**A fitness app ad:**
- Before (8s): Person struggling with morning runs. Tired. Giving up. Couch.
- After (8s): Same person, same running route. Energized. Smiling. PR time on their watch.
- Bridge (14s): The app — training plan, progress tracking, encouragement. "Get there. Your way."

## Common Pitfalls
- Before too mild (if the pain isn't felt, the After doesn't matter)
- After too unrealistic (aspirational ≠ fantasy. Keep it achievable.)
- Bridge too long (the contrast is the story — the bridge is the explanation, keep it tight)
