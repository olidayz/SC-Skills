# Three-Act Structure

## What It Is
The foundational Western narrative structure. Everything is setup, confrontation, resolution. Aristotle called it beginning, middle, end. Syd Field codified it for screenwriting. Almost every other framework is a variation of this.

## The Beats

### Act 1: Setup (25%)
- **Opening image:** Sets tone, world, and often mirrors the closing image
- **Ordinary world:** Who is the protagonist before everything changes?
- **Inciting incident:** The event that disrupts normal. Can't be ignored.
- **Debate/refusal:** Protagonist resists the call (briefly)
- **Act 1 turning point:** Protagonist commits. No going back.

### Act 2: Confrontation (50%)
- **Rising action:** Protagonist pursues goal, faces escalating obstacles
- **Midpoint:** A major shift — either a false victory or a devastating setback. The story pivots here. Stakes raise.
- **Complications:** Allies tested, enemies strengthen, plan falls apart
- **All Is Lost:** The lowest point. Everything has failed. The old approach is dead.
- **Dark Night of the Soul:** Protagonist must find something new within themselves

### Act 3: Resolution (25%)
- **Act 3 turning point:** New approach/insight/choice
- **Climax:** The final confrontation. The protagonist is tested on who they've become.
- **Resolution:** New equilibrium. The world after change.
- **Closing image:** Mirrors opening — shows transformation.

## When to Use It
- **Best for:** Features, long-form, any story that needs clear structure
- **Tone fit:** Universal — works for comedy, drama, thriller, anything
- **Format fit:** 60s+ ads (compressed), shorts (simplified), features (full), series (per episode)

## Example
**Finding Nemo:** Act 1: Marlin loses everything except Nemo, then Nemo is taken. Act 2: Marlin crosses the ocean, faces sharks, jellyfish, whales — transforms from fearful to brave. Midpoint: Marlin is inside the whale (literal belly of the beast). All Is Lost: Nemo appears dead. Act 3: Marlin lets go of control, trusts Nemo. Closing image mirrors opening but Marlin has changed.

## Common Pitfalls
- Act 2 sag: the middle 50% drags if obstacles don't escalate
- Passive protagonist: they must drive the action, not ride it
- Weak midpoint: if nothing fundamentally shifts at the midpoint, Act 2 feels like one long slog
