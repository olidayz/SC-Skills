# Nonlinear Narrative

## What It Is
Any story told out of chronological order. Flashbacks, flash-forwards, parallel timelines, fragmented structure, reverse chronology. The audience assembles the story like a puzzle. When done right, the structure IS the experience.

## Types

### Reverse Chronology
Events told backwards. Each scene explains the one before it.
- **Example:** Memento — each scene moves backwards, mirroring the protagonist's memory loss
- **Effect:** Turns "what happens next?" into "what happened before?"
- **Best for:** Mystery, revelation, recontextualization

### Fragmented / Mosaic
Scenes from different times, places, or perspectives, assembled non-sequentially.
- **Example:** Pulp Fiction — three storylines shuffled, creating meaning through juxtaposition
- **Effect:** Connections between fragments create the narrative. The audience does the work.
- **Best for:** Ensemble stories, thematic exploration, mood pieces

### Parallel Timelines
Two or more timelines running simultaneously, converging at the end.
- **Example:** Arrival — present and "flashbacks" revealed to be flash-forwards
- **Effect:** Dramatic irony, surprise, emotional depth
- **Best for:** Sci-fi, romance, stories about memory or time

### Frame Story
A story within a story. The outer narrative contextualizes the inner one.
- **Example:** The Princess Bride — grandpa reading to the kid frames the adventure
- **Effect:** Adds meta-commentary, emotional distance that can enhance intimacy
- **Best for:** Fairy tales, adaptations, stories about storytelling

### In Medias Res
Start in the middle of the action, then fill in how we got here.
- **Example:** Breaking Bad pilot — opens with Walt in his underwear in the desert, then rewinds
- **Effect:** Immediate hook. Audience thinks "how did we get HERE?"
- **Best for:** Thrillers, action, any story that needs to grab attention immediately

## When to Use It
- **Best for:** Stories where the order of revelation matters more than the order of events
- **Tone fit:** Thriller, mystery, art, philosophical, experimental
- **Format fit:** Features, shorts (especially In Medias Res), music videos (fragmented works beautifully)
- **Short-form gold:** In Medias Res is perfect for hooks. Open on the climax, rewind, arrive back.

## The Rule
Nonlinear structure must serve the story. If rearranging events doesn't add meaning, irony, or emotional impact — just tell it straight. Nonlinear for its own sake is self-indulgent.

## For AI Filmmaking
Nonlinear is actually easier to produce with AI tools because:
- Each scene/fragment can be generated independently
- No need for continuous character consistency across long sequences
- Visual style shifts between timelines are a feature, not a bug

## Common Pitfalls
- Confusion ≠ complexity. The audience should be intrigued, not lost.
- Each timeline/fragment needs its own internal logic
- The convergence point must be worth the puzzle
