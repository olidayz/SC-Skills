# The Hero's Journey

## What It Is
Joseph Campbell's monomyth — the story pattern found across cultures and centuries. Refined for screenwriters by Christopher Vogler. The protagonist leaves the ordinary world, enters the unknown, faces trials, transforms, and returns changed.

## The 12 Stages

1. **Ordinary World** — The hero's normal life. Establishes what's at stake if they change (and what's at stake if they don't).
2. **Call to Adventure** — Something disrupts normal. An invitation, a threat, a discovery.
3. **Refusal of the Call** — Fear, doubt, obligation. The hero resists. This makes them human.
4. **Meeting the Mentor** — A guide appears. Gives tools, advice, or courage. Not answers — tools.
5. **Crossing the Threshold** — The hero commits. Enters the special world. Point of no return.
6. **Tests, Allies, Enemies** — The hero learns the rules of the new world. Builds team. Faces obstacles.
7. **Approach to the Inmost Cave** — Preparation for the central ordeal. Tension builds.
8. **The Ordeal** — The central crisis. Death and rebirth (literal or metaphorical). The hero faces their greatest fear.
9. **Reward (Seizing the Sword)** — The hero gains what they came for. But it's not over.
10. **The Road Back** — Consequences of the ordeal. The special world pushes back.
11. **Resurrection** — The final test. The hero must prove they've truly changed. Highest stakes.
12. **Return with the Elixir** — The hero returns to the ordinary world, transformed. Brings something back for others.

## When to Use It
- **Best for:** Epic stories, transformation arcs, stories about growth and change
- **Tone fit:** Adventure, drama, fantasy, sci-fi, inspirational
- **Format fit:** Features (ideal), series (season arc), long shorts (3+ min), even 60s ads if heavily compressed (show the transformation)
- **Not ideal for:** Quick ads, comedy-first stories, ensemble pieces without a clear protagonist

## Example
**Star Wars (1977):** Luke's ordinary world (Tatooine farm) → Call (Leia's message) → Refusal ("I can't leave") → Mentor (Obi-Wan) → Threshold (leaving Tatooine) → Tests (Mos Eisley, Death Star) → Ordeal (trash compactor, Obi-Wan's death) → Reward (rescuing Leia) → Road Back (Death Star pursuit) → Resurrection (trench run, trusting the Force) → Elixir (medals, new family).

## Compression for Short-Form
You can use 3-4 stages for a short:
- Ordinary World (2s) → Call + Threshold (5s) → Ordeal (15s) → Return transformed (5s)
- Works for transformation stories, makeover content, "watch me try" formats

## Common Pitfalls
- Treating it as rigid formula instead of flexible template
- Mentor who gives too much — hero needs to earn their transformation
- Skipping the Refusal — without hesitation, the journey feels too easy
