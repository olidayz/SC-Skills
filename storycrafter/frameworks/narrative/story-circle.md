# Dan Harmon's Story Circle

## What It Is
Dan Harmon's simplified Hero's Journey — 8 steps arranged in a circle. Created for TV writing (Community, Rick and Morty) but works for anything. Elegant because it maps to a simple pattern: the character goes from comfort → desire → unfamiliar → change and back.

## The 8 Steps

1. **YOU** — A character in a zone of comfort (establish who they are)
2. **NEED** — But they want something (desire that drives the story)
3. **GO** — They enter an unfamiliar situation (crossing the threshold)
4. **SEARCH** — They adapt to it, struggle, learn (the middle of the journey)
5. **FIND** — They get what they wanted (but it comes with a cost)
6. **TAKE** — They pay a heavy price for it (the ordeal, the sacrifice)
7. **RETURN** — They go back to the familiar situation (but they've changed)
8. **CHANGE** — They are capable of change (transformation is proven)

## The Circle Logic
Draw a circle. Top = order/comfort. Bottom = chaos/unknown. Left side = descent. Right side = ascent.

- Steps 1-2: Top (comfort, but incomplete)
- Steps 3-4: Descending into the unknown
- Steps 5-6: Bottom (deepest in the unknown, highest stakes)
- Steps 7-8: Ascending back, transformed

The circle means you can start the next story right where this one ended. That's why it's perfect for episodic TV.

## When to Use It
- **Best for:** Episodic content, series, anything that needs repeatable structure
- **Tone fit:** Comedy, drama, genre — extremely versatile
- **Format fit:** TV episodes (designed for this), shorts (quick 8-step arc), series content, YouTube series
- **Strength:** Simple enough to hold in your head. You can story-circle a 30s short or a feature.

## Example
**Rick and Morty (any episode):** 1. Morty in his comfort zone. 2. Rick proposes/Morty wants something. 3. They go somewhere crazy. 4. Things spiral. 5. They find what they want. 6. It costs them something (usually emotionally). 7. They return home. 8. Morty is slightly different (usually more jaded).

## For Short-Form
The Story Circle compresses beautifully:
- Steps 1-2 (setup): 3-5 seconds
- Steps 3-4 (journey): 10-15 seconds
- Steps 5-6 (cost): 5-10 seconds
- Steps 7-8 (return): 3-5 seconds

Total: 30 seconds for a full character arc.

## Common Pitfalls
- Making NEED too vague — it should be specific and visual
- Skipping TAKE (the cost) — without sacrifice, transformation feels unearned
- Step 8 (CHANGE) can't just be stated — it has to be shown through action
