# The Reveal

## What It Is
Withhold one piece of information. Let the audience build assumptions. Then reveal the truth. The gap between what they assumed and what's real creates the emotional hit — surprise, delight, awe, or shock.

## How It Works

1. **Show, but hide.** Present a situation that seems clear but is actually incomplete.
2. **Build assumptions.** Let the audience fill in the gaps with their expectations.
3. **Maintain the illusion.** Everything you show should support the wrong conclusion.
4. **Reveal.** Pull back the curtain. Show what was really happening.
5. **Land the emotion.** The reveal should make the audience feel something — not just "oh, I was wrong" but "oh, that's BEAUTIFUL/TERRIFYING/HILARIOUS."

## Types of Reveals

### The Scale Reveal
What seemed small is actually huge (or vice versa).
- Miniature model → camera pulls out to show real city
- Close-up of "landscape" → reveals it's a painting on a grain of rice

### The Context Reveal
The situation isn't what you thought.
- Someone "breaking in" to a house → it's their own house, they forgot their keys
- A "fight scene" → actually a dance rehearsal

### The Identity Reveal
The character isn't who you assumed.
- The "old man" is actually the child from the opening, decades later
- The narrator is the villain

### The Process Reveal
How something was made. Deeply satisfying.
- A beautiful painting → time-lapse reveals the whole process
- A meal → reveal the 12-hour cook from scratch

### The Transformation Reveal
Before → After with the middle hidden.
- Dirty room → cut to black → pristine room
- Raw material → finished product

## When to Use It
- **Best for:** Content that depends on surprise, curiosity, or awe
- **Tone fit:** Any — comedy (misdirection + punchline), drama (twist), awe (scale/beauty)
- **Format fit:** Perfect for shorts (the whole format is a setup for the reveal), ads (product reveal), transformations
- **Works on every platform** because curiosity is universal

## Timing (30s)
- Setup (building assumptions): 18-22s
- Reveal: 3-5s
- Reaction/landing: 3-5s

**The ratio matters.** More setup = bigger payoff. But too much setup on short-form = lost audience. Find the sweet spot for the platform.

## Example
**A hypothetical 30s short:**
- We see hands carefully crafting something. Intense focus. Small tools. (15s)
- Details: painting, gluing, positioning tiny elements. (8s)
- Camera pulls out: it's a perfect miniature replica of the room we're IN. (5s)
- Final beat: the miniature has a tiny version of itself on the desk. Inception. (2s)

## Common Pitfalls
- Reveal is underwhelming (setup promises more than the reveal delivers)
- Setup is boring (you need micro-hooks during the build to maintain attention)
- Reveal is too early (resist the urge — milk the setup)
- No emotional payoff (surprise alone isn't enough — the audience needs to FEEL something)
