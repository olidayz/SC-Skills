# Pattern Interrupt

## What It Is
The scroll-stopper. You break the expected visual, audio, or narrative pattern in the first 1-2 seconds. The brain's prediction engine gets disrupted, and attention snaps into focus. "That's not what I expected" = you have 5 more seconds to hook them.

## How It Works

The human brain is a prediction machine. It constantly anticipates what comes next. When a prediction fails — something unexpected appears — the brain allocates attention to understand why. That's your window.

## Types of Pattern Interrupts

### Visual Interrupt
Something in the frame shouldn't be there.
- A person in a suit standing in a swimming pool
- Food that's the wrong color
- Scale distortion (tiny person, giant object)
- AI filmmaking gold: surreal visuals are easy to generate

### Audio Interrupt
Sound that doesn't match the visual.
- Calm scene + sudden loud sound
- Serious visuals + absurd narration
- Silence where music is expected

### Narrative Interrupt
The story breaks its own rules.
- "This is a cooking video" → it's clearly not
- Starting with the ending
- Breaking the fourth wall immediately
- "Don't watch this" (reverse psychology)

### Contextual Interrupt
Content that doesn't belong on the platform.
- Cinema-quality visuals on TikTok
- A "documentary" about something absurd
- Corporate presentation style about a sandwich

## When to Use It
- **Best for:** First 1-3 seconds of any short-form content. Often combined with other frameworks for the full arc.
- **Tone fit:** Surreal, comedic, shocking, mysterious
- **Format fit:** TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts — anything fighting the scroll
- **Limitation:** Pattern Interrupt is a HOOK, not a structure. You need another framework for the remaining 27 seconds.

## Combo Patterns
- Pattern Interrupt → PAS (hook with surprise, then problem-agitate-solve)
- Pattern Interrupt → The Reveal (hook with surprise, then build to reveal)
- Pattern Interrupt → Kishōtenketsu (surprise opening IS the "ten" twist, then recontextualize)

## Example
**A hypothetical coffee brand (30s):**
- Pattern Interrupt (2s): A goldfish is sitting at a desk, typing. (What?)
- Interest builds (8s): The goldfish is clearly tired. Yawns. Checks clock.
- Solution (10s): Someone places a cup of coffee next to the bowl. The fish perks up.
- Payoff (10s): Cut to human at same desk, same posture, same coffee. "We get it. Mondays."

## Common Pitfalls
- Interrupt unrelated to the story (clickbait — audience feels cheated)
- Too subtle (if they don't notice, it didn't work)
- All interrupt, no follow-through (you stopped the scroll but said nothing)
