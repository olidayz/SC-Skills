# Pitch Format

## Types of Pitches

### Logline (1 sentence)
The entire story in one sentence. This is the hardest thing to write and the most important.

**Formula:** When [INCITING INCIDENT] happens to [PROTAGONIST], they must [OBJECTIVE] before [STAKES/DEADLINE], but [OBSTACLE] stands in the way.

**Tests:**
- Does it have a protagonist with a clear goal?
- Is there opposition/conflict?
- Are stakes implied or stated?
- Does it make you want to know what happens?
- Could this ONLY be this story? (If your logline could describe 10 different movies, it's too vague.)

**Examples of great loglines:**
- Jaws: "A police chief must protect his beach town from a great white shark despite political pressure to keep the beaches open."
- Toy Story: "A cowboy doll is threatened by the arrival of a spaceman action figure as his owner's favorite toy."

### Elevator Pitch (30 seconds / 3 sentences)
1. The hook (logline, compressed)
2. The journey (what happens)
3. Why it matters (why THIS story, why NOW)

### Treatment (1-3 pages)
- Logline
- Synopsis (beginning, middle, end — don't hide the ending)
- Tone and visual references
- Character descriptions (1-2 lines each)
- Why this project (personal connection, market gap, timeliness)

### Pitch Deck (10-15 slides)
1. Title + logline + key art
2. The world (visual mood board)
3. The story (3-5 beat summary)
4. Characters (with visual references)
5. Tone + comparables ("X meets Y")
6. Visual style (reference images, color palette)
7. Audience + market
8. Why now
9. Team (if relevant)
10. Ask (what you need)

### Verbal Pitch (5-10 minutes)
- Open with the hook — not "So my story is about..." but the moment that makes them lean in.
- Tell the story like you're telling a friend about a movie you loved.
- Hit the key beats, don't summarize everything.
- End with the climax and theme — make them FEEL the ending.
- Leave silence. Let it land.

## Pitch Craft Rules

1. **Comparables are shorthand, not crutch.** "X meets Y" should illuminate, not replace your description. Pick unexpected combos. "Jaws meets The Office" says more than "an action-comedy."
2. **Know your audience.** Pitching to Netflix ≠ pitching to a brand ≠ pitching to investors. Adjust emphasis.
3. **Confidence > polish.** A rough pitch delivered with conviction beats a polished pitch delivered with apology.
4. **Show, don't describe.** In a deck, one perfect reference image beats a paragraph of description.
5. **The "so what?" test.** After every sentence, imagine them thinking "so what?" If you can't answer, cut it.
6. **Never apologize.** "I know this might not be for everyone" = dead on arrival.

## Output for Pitches

Based on what the user needs:
1. Logline (always — it's the foundation)
2. Elevator pitch version
3. Treatment (if requested)
4. Pitch deck outline with slide descriptions
5. Verbal pitch script (if requested)
6. Comparable suggestions with rationale
