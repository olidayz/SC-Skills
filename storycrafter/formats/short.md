# Short-Form Format

## The Physics of Short-Form Storytelling

Shorts (15s–3min) are the dominant storytelling format of the 2020s. They reward compression, visual hooks, and emotional density. The audience's thumb is always hovering over "scroll." Your job: make them forget they can leave.

## Platform Differences

### TikTok (9:16 vertical)
- Hook: 1-2 seconds or you're dead
- Sweet spot: 30-60s for story, 15-30s for concept
- Rewatch value = algorithm boost. The Loop framework thrives here.
- Sound is ON (unlike ads). Music choice is half the story.
- Trending audio can carry a mediocre concept. Great concept + right audio = viral.
- Comments drive reach. Stories that provoke "wait WHAT" or debate win.

### Instagram Reels (9:16 vertical)
- Aesthetic matters more than TikTok. Visual polish expected.
- 30-90s sweet spot.
- First frame matters — it's the thumbnail.
- Carousel + reel combos can extend story across formats.
- Less raw, more produced than TikTok.

### YouTube Shorts (9:16 vertical)
- Can be up to 3 minutes.
- More room for setup than TikTok.
- Subscribe CTA integration matters.
- YouTube audience expects slightly higher production value.
- Search/discovery different from TikTok — titles and descriptions matter.

## Short-Form Storytelling Rules

1. **3-second rule.** If the viewer doesn't know why they should stay by second 3, they're gone.
2. **One concept.** Don't try to tell two stories. Compression demands focus.
3. **End strong.** The last 2-3 seconds determine shares. Surprise, punchline, emotional hit, or cliffhanger.
4. **Visual-first.** Can someone understand this on mute? (Less critical than ads — sound is usually on — but still a good test.)
5. **Rewatch design.** Can you plant something in the first shot that only makes sense after the ending? That's Loop territory.
6. **Music is structure.** The beat drop, the build, the silence — these are your act breaks.

## Emotional Density

Short-form demands what feature filmmakers call "emotional density" — the ratio of feeling to runtime. In a feature, you can breathe. In a short, every second carries emotional weight.

Map your emotional arc even for 30s:
- Seconds 1-5: curiosity/tension (WHY am I watching?)
- Seconds 5-15: escalation (WHAT is happening?)
- Seconds 15-25: peak (the HIT — laugh, gasp, cry, awe)
- Seconds 25-30: resolution/hook (SHARE moment or rewatch trigger)

## Best Frameworks for Shorts

| Framework | Why it works | Example type |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| The Loop | Rewatch = algorithm gold | Mystery, twist endings |
| The Reveal | Curiosity holds through | Transformation, before/after |
| The Transformation | Visual satisfaction | Makeover, process, creation |
| Pattern Interrupt | Scroll-stopping | Surreal, unexpected, absurd |
| Story Spine | Quick full arc | Mini-narratives, character pieces |
| Kishōtenketsu | No conflict needed | Aesthetic, meditative, surprising |

## Output for Shorts

When outlining a short, always deliver:
1. Hook description (first 3 seconds — what do we SEE?)
2. Beat sheet (timed to seconds, matched to music if possible)
3. Emotional arc mapped to runtime
4. Visual style note (aesthetic, raw, cinematic, surreal, etc.)
5. Music/sound direction
6. Platform-specific notes (vertical framing, text overlay placement, CTA if needed)
7. Rewatch potential assessment (is there a loop? A hidden detail? A "wait, go back" moment?)
