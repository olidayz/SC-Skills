# Long-Form Format

## Scope

Features (80-120min), series (episodic), documentaries, music videos (3-6min), and anything over 3 minutes that tells a sustained story.

## Features / Films

### Structure
Most features use 3-act structure as the backbone, with other frameworks layered on top:
- **Act 1** (25%): Setup, ordinary world, inciting incident
- **Act 2** (50%): Confrontation, rising stakes, midpoint shift, all is lost
- **Act 3** (25%): Resolution, climax, new equilibrium

### Key Beats
Use Save the Cat or Hero's Journey for detailed beat mapping. But every feature needs at minimum:
1. Opening image (sets tone, often mirrors closing image)
2. Inciting incident (the thing that breaks normal)
3. Midpoint (raises stakes, shifts direction — not just "more of the same")
4. All Is Lost moment (darkest point)
5. Climax (the choice that defines the character)
6. Closing image (shows transformation)

### Common Mistakes
- Act 2 sag — the middle 50% is where most stories die. Every scene needs either rising tension or deepening character. If it does neither, cut it.
- Passive protagonist — the hero must DRIVE the story, not be dragged through it.
- Theme stated too early — show it, let the audience discover it.

## Series / Episodic

### Episode Structure
Each episode is a mini-movie with its own arc, PLUS advances the season arc:
- **Episode arc:** Setup → complication → resolution (or cliffhanger)
- **Season arc:** Macro story that builds episode to episode
- **Series arc:** Character transformation across seasons

### Pilot Essentials
The pilot must do five things:
1. Establish the world (where are we?)
2. Introduce the protagonist (who do we follow?)
3. Present the central question (what's at stake?)
4. Demonstrate the show's tone and format (what will watching feel like?)
5. Hook for episode 2 (why come back?)

### Episode Types
- **Bottle episode:** Single location, character-focused, budget-friendly
- **Mythology episode:** Advances the big arc significantly
- **Standalone:** Complete story, light arc advancement
- **Premiere/Finale:** High stakes, high production, major turns

## Documentary

### Structures
- **Chronological:** Events in order. Simple but can be flat.
- **Thematic:** Organized by theme/argument. More engaging.
- **Character-driven:** Follow a person's journey. Most compelling.
- **Mystery/investigation:** The filmmaker discovers truth alongside the audience.

### Documentary Storytelling Rules
1. Real life doesn't have act breaks — you impose structure in the edit.
2. The best docs have a clear question driving them. Not a topic — a question.
3. Talking heads are not story. They're exposition. Use sparingly.
4. Verité (observation footage) is where the real story lives.

## Music Video

### Structures
- **Narrative:** Tells a story that complements/contrasts the lyrics
- **Performance:** Artist performing, visually styled
- **Conceptual:** Abstract visual interpretation of the music
- **Hybrid:** Mix of the above (most common)

### Music Video Rules
1. The song IS the structure. Verse/chorus/bridge = your act breaks.
2. Visual rhythm should match or intentionally counter musical rhythm.
3. The drop/chorus needs visual escalation.
4. Repetition is your friend — recurring visual motifs build meaning.

## AI Filmmaking Considerations for Long-Form

- **Character consistency** is the biggest challenge. Plan for it.
- **Scene transitions** need planning — AI tools handle cuts better than continuous shots.
- **Dialogue scenes** are hard in AI. Consider visual storytelling with VO.
- **Style consistency** across 5+ minutes requires a locked visual direction.
- Break long-form into modular scenes that can be generated independently.

## Output for Long-Form

1. Logline
2. Synopsis (1 page)
3. Full beat sheet (framework-specific)
4. Character profiles (want/need/wound for each major character)
5. Thematic statement
6. Tone references ("this meets that")
7. Act structure with emotional arc
8. Production considerations
