# Ad Format

## The Physics of Ad Storytelling

Ads don't have the luxury of patience. You have seconds — sometimes 3, sometimes 60 — to make someone feel something AND remember your product. Story structure still applies, but compressed to its essence.

## Duration Variants

### 15s (Pre-roll / Bumper)
- ONE beat. That's it.
- Hook (1-2s) → Single emotional moment (8-10s) → Brand/CTA (3-5s)
- Best frameworks: Pattern Interrupt, The Reveal, Before/After Bridge
- Think of it as a single joke, a single gasp, a single "wait what?"

### 30s (Standard)
- Two to three beats max.
- Hook (2-3s) → Problem/tension (10s) → Resolution + brand (10s) → CTA (5s)
- Best frameworks: PAS, AIDA, The Transformation
- This is where most story-driven ads live. Enough room for a mini-arc.

### 60s (Long-form ad / branded content)
- Full mini-story possible.
- Can use Story Spine, simplified Hero's Journey, or Save the Cat compressed
- Hook (3-5s) → Setup (10s) → Conflict (15s) → Resolution (15s) → Brand + CTA (10-15s)
- Danger zone: 60s is long enough to bore people. Every second must earn its place.

## Ad Storytelling Rules

1. **The product is not the hero.** The customer is. The product is the mentor/tool/magic sword.
2. **Hook is non-negotiable.** If you lose them in 3 seconds, nothing else matters.
3. **One emotion per ad.** Don't try to be funny AND inspiring AND sad. Pick one. Nail it.
4. **The brand reveal should feel earned.** Not slapped on. The story should make the brand inevitable.
5. **Sound-off first.** Most ads are seen muted. Does your story work visually? If not, rethink.
6. **CTA is story resolution.** "Shop now" lands harder when it's the natural next step, not an interruption.

## Hook Types for Ads

- **Question hook:** "What if your dog could talk?" (curiosity gap)
- **Conflict hook:** Open on the problem. Immediate tension.
- **Visual pattern interrupt:** Something unexpected in frame 1.
- **Statement hook:** Bold claim. "This changed everything."
- **Social proof hook:** "10 million people already..." (tribe instinct)

## When to Use Which Framework

| Framework | Best for | Why |
|-----------|----------|-----|
| AIDA | Direct response, conversion | Linear persuasion arc |
| PAS | Problem-aware audience | Agitation creates urgency |
| Before/After/Bridge | Transformation stories | Visual contrast is powerful |
| Significant Object | Brand storytelling | Emotional attachment to product |
| Pattern Interrupt | Scroll-stopping | Breaks expectation in 1s |
| The Reveal | Mystery/surprise ads | Curiosity holds attention |

## Output for Ads

When outlining an ad, always deliver:
1. Hook type + first 3 seconds described
2. Beat sheet (timed to the second)
3. Visual description for each beat (think: storyboard-ready)
4. Sound design notes (music, SFX, VO or no VO)
5. Brand integration moment
6. CTA placement and type
