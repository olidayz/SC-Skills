# Jung's 12 Archetypes

## What They Are
Carl Jung identified universal character patterns that appear across all cultures and stories. These aren't character templates — they're psychological engines. Each archetype has a core desire, a greatest fear, and a signature behavior. Use them to build characters with built-in depth.

## The 12 Archetypes

### 1. The Innocent
- **Desire:** Safety, happiness, paradise
- **Fear:** Punishment, doing something wrong
- **Strategy:** Do things right, be good
- **Gift:** Faith, optimism
- **Shadow:** Denial, naivety
- **Example:** Forrest Gump, Dorothy (Wizard of Oz), Wall-E

### 2. The Orphan (Everyman)
- **Desire:** Belonging, connection
- **Fear:** Being left out, standing out
- **Strategy:** Blend in, be solid and real
- **Gift:** Empathy, realism
- **Shadow:** Victim mentality, cynicism
- **Example:** Frodo, John McClane, the audience surrogate in any story

### 3. The Hero
- **Desire:** Mastery, proving worth through courage
- **Fear:** Weakness, vulnerability
- **Strategy:** Be strong, competent, powerful
- **Gift:** Courage, competence
- **Shadow:** Arrogance, obsession with winning
- **Example:** Luke Skywalker, Wonder Woman, Rocky

### 4. The Caregiver
- **Desire:** Protect and care for others
- **Fear:** Selfishness, ingratitude
- **Strategy:** Do things for others
- **Gift:** Compassion, generosity
- **Shadow:** Martyrdom, enabling
- **Example:** Samwise Gamgee, Mary Poppins, Baymax

### 5. The Explorer
- **Desire:** Freedom, discovery
- **Fear:** Being trapped, conformity
- **Strategy:** Journey, seek, experience
- **Gift:** Autonomy, ambition
- **Shadow:** Aimlessness, never settling
- **Example:** Indiana Jones, Moana, Into the Wild's Chris McCandless

### 6. The Rebel (Outlaw)
- **Desire:** Revolution, liberation
- **Fear:** Being powerless, conforming
- **Strategy:** Disrupt, destroy, shock
- **Gift:** Freedom, radical change
- **Shadow:** Nihilism, criminality
- **Example:** Tyler Durden, V (V for Vendetta), Han Solo

### 7. The Lover
- **Desire:** Intimacy, connection, beauty
- **Fear:** Being alone, unloved, unwanted
- **Strategy:** Become attractive, create connection
- **Gift:** Passion, commitment, appreciation
- **Shadow:** Obsession, jealousy, loss of identity
- **Example:** Romeo, Rose (Titanic), Amélie

### 8. The Creator
- **Desire:** Create something of enduring value
- **Fear:** Mediocrity, failed vision
- **Strategy:** Develop skill, artistic control
- **Gift:** Creativity, imagination
- **Shadow:** Perfectionism, self-indulgence
- **Example:** Willy Wonka, Tony Stark (as inventor), Ratatouille's Remy

### 9. The Jester
- **Desire:** Joy, lightness, living in the moment
- **Fear:** Boredom, being boring
- **Strategy:** Play, make jokes, be funny
- **Gift:** Joy, perspective through humor
- **Shadow:** Cruelty, wasted potential, avoidance
- **Example:** The Joker (some versions), Timon & Pumbaa, Deadpool

### 10. The Sage
- **Desire:** Truth, understanding
- **Fear:** Ignorance, being deceived
- **Strategy:** Think, analyze, learn
- **Gift:** Wisdom, intelligence
- **Shadow:** Ivory tower, disconnection from reality
- **Example:** Gandalf, Yoda, Morpheus

### 11. The Magician
- **Desire:** Transformation, making dreams real
- **Fear:** Unintended negative consequences
- **Strategy:** Develop vision and live it
- **Gift:** Finding win-win solutions, transformation
- **Shadow:** Manipulation, becoming the villain
- **Example:** Doctor Strange, Dumbledore, The Wizard of Oz

### 12. The Ruler
- **Desire:** Control, order, power
- **Fear:** Chaos, being overthrown
- **Strategy:** Lead, create structure, take charge
- **Gift:** Responsibility, leadership
- **Shadow:** Tyranny, inability to delegate
- **Example:** T'Challa, Miranda Priestly, Mufasa

## How to Use Them

1. **Pick a primary archetype** for your protagonist — this is their core drive.
2. **Pick a shadow aspect** — this is their flaw, their danger zone.
3. **The character arc** is often the journey from shadow to gift (or gift to shadow for villains).
4. **Supporting characters** should represent contrasting archetypes — this creates natural tension.
5. **Villains** are often the shadow version of the hero's archetype, or a complementary archetype twisted.

## For Short-Form
You don't have time to develop — but you can SIGNAL an archetype in 2 seconds:
- Visual coding (leather jacket = Rebel, white coat = Sage, crown = Ruler)
- One line of dialogue that reveals the core desire
- A single choice that shows who they are
