# Want / Need / Wound

## What It Is
The most powerful character engine in storytelling. Three interconnected elements that drive everything a character does, why they fail, and how they grow.

## The Three Elements

### WANT (External Goal)
What the character consciously pursues. It's visible, stated, and drives the plot.
- "I want to find the treasure"
- "I want to win the competition"
- "I want to get the promotion"

**Rules:**
- Must be specific and concrete (not "I want to be happy")
- Must be achievable within the story
- The audience should understand WHY they want it
- This is what creates PLOT

### NEED (Internal Truth)
What the character actually needs to learn/become to be whole. They usually don't know this at the start. The gap between Want and Need IS the character arc.
- Want: find the treasure. Need: learn that family is the real treasure (cliché but clear)
- Want: win the competition. Need: learn they don't need external validation.
- Want: get the promotion. Need: learn to value themselves outside of work.

**Rules:**
- The character is usually WRONG about what will make them whole
- The Need contradicts or complicates the Want
- Achieving the Want without the Need leads to a hollow victory
- This is what creates THEME

### WOUND (Backstory Trauma)
The past experience that created the gap between Want and Need. The reason they're pursuing the wrong thing. The thing they're running from.
- They want the treasure because they grew up poor and equate money with safety (Wound: childhood poverty)
- They want to win because their parent always told them they weren't good enough (Wound: parental rejection)
- They want the promotion because their identity is tied to achievement after a personal loss (Wound: grief channeled into work)

**Rules:**
- The Wound creates the Want (it's the reason they're chasing the wrong goal)
- The Wound is what makes the Need scary (accepting the Need means facing the Wound)
- You don't always need to SHOW the Wound — but you need to know it as the writer
- This is what creates EMPATHY

## How They Work Together

```
WOUND (past) → creates → WANT (present goal) → blocks → NEED (real growth)
```

The story forces the character to confront the Wound, which is the only way to release the Want and embrace the Need.

**The climactic choice** in most great stories is: does the character choose the Want or the Need?
- Choose the Need: Growth. Transformation. (Most stories.)
- Choose the Want: Tragedy. The character doesn't change. (Breaking Bad, Citizen Kane.)

## Quick Character Build

For any character, answer these three questions:
1. **What do they want?** (Specific. External. Visible.)
2. **What do they need?** (Internal. Invisible to them. Theme-level.)
3. **What wound made them this way?** (Past. The root cause.)

If you can answer all three, you have a character that can drive a story.

## For Short-Form
In a 30s short, you can't develop all three — but you can IMPLY them:
- Show the Want in the first 5 seconds (what are they doing?)
- Show the Need in the climax (what do they realize?)
- The Wound stays implied (the audience fills it in)

## Examples

**Carl (Up):**
- Want: Fly the house to Paradise Falls (Ellie's dream)
- Need: Let go of Ellie and live a new life
- Wound: Ellie's death and the promise he couldn't keep

**Walter White (Breaking Bad):**
- Want: Provide for his family (money)
- Need: Accept mortality and his own ordinariness
- Wound: Lost his share of a billion-dollar company, lives with regret
- He chooses the Want. That's why it's a tragedy.

**Moana:**
- Want: Restore the heart of Te Fiti (external quest)
- Need: Discover who she truly is (identity beyond duty)
- Wound: Torn between her island duty and her ocean calling
