# Story Breakdowns — Real-World Examples

Analysis of great ads and shorts showing frameworks in action.

---

## 1. Apple — "1984" (60s, Super Bowl)

**Format:** TV Ad (60s)
**Framework:** Hero's Journey (compressed) + Pattern Interrupt
**Audience:** Mass market, tech-forward
**Controlling Idea:** "Freedom is achieved when one person dares to think differently."

**Beat Breakdown:**
- 0-15s: Ordinary World — Grey, dystopian. Workers march in lockstep. (Kishōtenketsu's Ki — pure establishment, no conflict yet.)
- 15-35s: The Journey — A woman in color runs through the grey world. She carries a hammer. Guards chase her. (Rising action.)
- 35-50s: The Ordeal — Big Brother on screen, preaching conformity. The woman sprints toward the screen. (Maximum tension.)
- 50-57s: Climax — She throws the hammer. The screen explodes. The drones' faces change. (Catharsis.)
- 57-60s: Resolution — Text: "On January 24th, Apple Computer will introduce Macintosh. And you'll see why 1984 won't be like '1984'."

**Why it works:** One character, one action, one metaphor. The product is never shown. The story IS the brand argument. The Pattern Interrupt is the color runner in a grey world.

## 2. Volvo Trucks — "The Epic Split" (75s)

**Format:** Online ad / branded content
**Framework:** The Reveal + Transformation
**Audience:** Industry + viral general audience
**Controlling Idea:** "Precision is proven through the impossible."

**Beat Breakdown:**
- 0-10s: Setup — Jean-Claude Van Damme's face. VO about precision and being tested.
- 10-30s: Development — Camera slowly pulls back. We see he's standing between two objects.
- 30-50s: The Reveal — The full picture: he's doing a split between TWO MOVING TRUCKS. Driving backwards.
- 50-75s: The Payoff — The split widens. Enya plays. Dawn breaks. Text: "Volvo Dynamic Steering."

**Why it works:** The Reveal is perfectly paced — you know something impressive is coming but the scale exceeds expectation. The product demo IS the spectacle. No separation between entertainment and message.

## 3. Duolingo TikTok (15-30s, ongoing series)

**Format:** Short-form social (TikTok/Reels)
**Framework:** Pattern Interrupt + Story Circle (micro)
**Audience:** Gen Z
**Controlling Idea:** "Learning is fun when the teacher is unhinged."

**Recurring Structure:**
- Hook (1-2s): Duo the owl in an unexpected situation (office, club, therapy)
- Escalation (5-15s): Duo does something increasingly unhinged related to learning
- Payoff (3-5s): Punchline + implicit brand message (learn or else)

**Why it works:** Each video is a micro Story Circle (Duo in comfort → Duo wants something → chaos → return). The Pattern Interrupt is a language learning mascot behaving like a chaotic entity. Brand personality IS content.

## 4. "The Neighbor's Window" — Oscar-winning short (20 min)

**Format:** Short film
**Framework:** Kishōtenketsu + The Reveal
**Audience:** Film festival / cinephile
**Controlling Idea:** "Envy is destroyed when you see the full picture of someone else's life."

**Beat Breakdown:**
- Ki: A woman watches her young, attractive neighbors through their window. They party, dance, make love.
- Shō: She becomes obsessed. Compares their life to her own (messy apartment, tired husband, screaming kids).
- Ten (Twist): She sees the young man is sick. Very sick. The parties were him living his final months.
- Ketsu: He dies. The young woman, now grieving, looks across and sees the protagonist's family — warm, alive, together. They envy each other.

**Why it works:** Pure Kishōtenketsu — no antagonist, no conflict. Just a shift in perspective that recontextualizes everything. The Reveal isn't a twist — it's a deepening.

## 5. Nike — "You Can't Stop Us" (90s)

**Format:** Brand film
**Framework:** Transformation + Before/After/Bridge (layered)
**Audience:** Universal / athletes
**Controlling Idea:** "Unity is proven when different bodies move as one."

**Beat Breakdown:**
- Split-screen throughout: two different athletes, matched in movement. A swimmer's stroke mirrors a skateboarder's push. A basketball dribble mirrors a soccer volley.
- The "before" is division (different sports, different bodies, different worlds)
- The "bridge" is the edit itself — the visual proof that movement is universal
- The "after" is unity — the final shots bring split screens together

**Why it works:** The technique IS the argument. No need for dialogue or explanation. The editing proves the theme visually. Every split-screen pair is a micro Before/After/Bridge.

## 6. Coca-Cola — "Masterpiece" (60s, AI-assisted)

**Format:** TV Ad (60s)
**Framework:** Hero's Journey (compressed) + Significant Object
**Audience:** Mass market
**Controlling Idea:** "Inspiration flows through connection."

**Beat Breakdown:**
- Ordinary World (5s): Art museum. Famous paintings hanging.
- Call (5s): A student stares at a blank canvas, stuck.
- Journey (30s): A Coke bottle passes through famous paintings — each artwork "hands" it to the next. Vermeer to Van Gogh to Warhol to street art.
- Reward (10s): The bottle emerges from the last painting, reaches the student.
- Return (10s): Student drinks, picks up brush, begins painting. Inspired.

**Why it works:** Significant Object framework — the Coke bottle absorbs meaning from every masterpiece it touches. By the time it reaches the student, it's not soda — it's inspiration. The product literally travels through art history.
